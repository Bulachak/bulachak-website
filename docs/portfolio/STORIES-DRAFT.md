## Agents 4 Athletes

Page title: More time for the work of sport

Plain definition: Agents 4 Athletes is an early-stage business building AI-supported operations for athletes, clubs, and sports organizations.

### Final page copy

I have met the same problem as an athlete, a club operator, and a committee chair. People who know their sport spend too much of their working day piecing together the administration around it. A booking sits in one place, a payment in another. Someone has to remember the next step.

Agents 4 Athletes grows out of that experience. I want an athlete starting a business to have a working back office. A coach should be able to find the information needed for tomorrow's session without reconstructing it from messages. A sports organization should be able to see who owns a task and whether it has been completed.

I am building A4A around coordinated AI agents that help carry out that work. The planned roles cover equipment sourcing, routine administration, and communications. A coordinating agent brings the work into one place. People set the rules, review consequential decisions, and keep responsibility for the promises made to athletes and families.

The name has a small technical joke inside it. A2A means agent-to-agent, a way for software agents to communicate. A4A puts the athlete in the name because that is who the technology is meant to serve. You should not need to understand the joke to find the service useful.

My earlier projects give this work a practical starting point. They include a bilingual PlayLA resource app, tools for a fencing academy, and governance design carried out as a Hall of Fame committee chair. Each case explains what I did and what evidence is available.

A4A itself is still developing, with prototypes and first paid pilots ahead. The aim is to test a few useful outcomes, learn what people will pay for, and build from there. Adaptive sport is a priority. I have also pledged 4% of future A4A commercial revenue to Parafencing 4 Progress, keeping the reason I started inside the business I am building.

Suggested next links: Explore sports-related work; Read the parafencing story; Talk about an operations problem.

## Parafencing 4 Progress

Page title: Help people start. Give them reasons to stay.

Plain definition: Parafencing 4 Progress is the initiative I am developing to improve access to wheelchair fencing through equipment, coaching, and dependable support.

### Final page copy

Parafencing is the reason I started building this business. I wanted a way to support the sport that could keep going: something with practical organization behind it and a source of funding that could grow over time.

Wheelchair fencing needs specialized equipment and people who know how to use it. A club considering a program has practical questions before an athlete ever arrives. What equipment does it need? Where can it obtain it? Who can coach the session, and how will the program continue after an introductory event?

Parafencing 4 Progress is my attempt to work on those questions together. The vision includes clearer equipment comparisons and sourcing, starter resources for clubs, and coaching material people can use in practice. Camps and athlete development belong alongside that groundwork. Access includes the support that makes it possible to come back.

Some of my earlier work offers methods we can learn from. An inventory system can make equipment easier to account for and distribute. A clear program page can help a family understand where to go and what to expect. Those are useful starting points for design; their value to parafencing still needs to be tested in parafencing settings.

I am developing P4P as Bulachak's nonprofit leg. The organizational form and funding arrangements are still taking shape. I have pledged 4% of future A4A commercial revenue to this work. That is a commitment for the business as it earns revenue, not a report of money already distributed.

The ambition is a sport supported by people who can share knowledge and resources without having to rebuild the same foundations in every club. The next work is practical: identify what a club or athlete needs, develop the right support with the people who will use it, and make responsibilities clear enough that the work can continue. I want the care put into starting a program to have somewhere to go after the first session.

Suggested next links: Explore methods that may support P4P; Share a club or equipment need; Read how A4A connects.

## Immigrant 4 Immigrant

Page title: Pass the help forward

Plain definition: Immigrant 4 Immigrant is an initiative for immigrants to support one another's businesses, share knowledge, and sustain their communities.

### Final page copy

When I first imagined Bulachak, I was thinking about partnerships among immigrant communities. As my work in sport grew, I wanted to keep room for the people building businesses and organizations outside that world. Their work matters to me too.

Immigrant 4 Immigrant gives that purpose a name. I4I sounds like "eye for eye." It deliberately turns a phrase about retaliation into an invitation to help one another. Knowledge, introductions, and practical support can pass between people and across generations. I want the name to remind us that what we pass on can make somebody else's life easier.

My Tatar identity is part of this story. I have imagined a small economy of people who know what others are making, recommend one another's work, and find reasons to collaborate. That can begin within a cultural community and reach beyond it. There should be room for a business to grow while the language and knowledge around it stay alive.

My work with the American Bashqort & Tatar Association, Tuganlyq, is one concrete part of that story. I supported its documentation, governance, and digital presence. A cultural organization needs a way to explain its purpose, maintain its information, and share responsibility for what happens next. Those everyday arrangements help people carry a collective project forward.

I4I is still taking shape as an initiative. I want to develop useful connections between people who have something to offer each other: a founder who understands a local market, a practitioner with a skill another business needs, or a community group with knowledge worth preserving. Clear expectations and fair exchanges make those connections easier to trust.

Some of this work will overlap with Agents 4 Athletes. An immigrant may also be an athlete opening an academy or building a business. I want Bulachak to make those connections easier to find, so a person can draw on the experience that fits their situation and, in time, offer something back.

Suggested next links: Read the Tuganlyq case; Explore community work; Start a conversation.

## Founder

Page title: Kamilla Gafurzianova, OLY

Plain definition: Olympic silver medalist in fencing, coach, and systems builder; founder of Bulachak.

### Final page copy

I am Kamilla Gafurzianova, an Olympic silver medalist in fencing, a coach, and a systems builder. I've worked in sport as an athlete, operator, and committee chair. Each role shapes how I approach the practical work behind sport.

I build websites and tools, organize information, and work through the decisions behind them. My portfolio includes youth-sport resources, operations projects, brand tools, and community governance. I like getting the details into a form someone else can understand and use.

My method is called PPPP: Past, Present, Possible, Practice. I begin with the history and the actual work, look at what could improve, then turn decisions into something people can repeat. Clear ownership matters throughout.

Bulachak brings that practice together with my reasons for doing it. I want to help build parafencing, support people working in sport, and create useful partnerships among immigrant communities. My Tatar identity is part of that perspective.

I care about excellence and how people are treated while pursuing it. I want the tools I build to help them keep doing good work with more understanding and less avoidable effort. The projects here show what I have made, what I have learned, and what I am still developing.
