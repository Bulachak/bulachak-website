# Purpose and limits

Four complete article bodies were read on 12 September 2026, including their endings, rather than relying on search snippets. This is a small, deliberately selected reading sample of interviews and community reporting. It is not a representative corpus, a native-speaker review, or evidence that the current Tatar translation is correct. Published dialogue is edited journalism, not a recording of unmediated conversation.

The notes concern wording and register. They do not supply Bulachak's philosophy, establish its activities, or authorize changing its names. The Emerald Pasture, its co-opetition concept, and the three branches remain governed by Kamilla's source material and corrections. No application copy was edited during this research.

# Articles read

## 1. Adult language learning across countries

**[Әти теле](https://syuyumbike.ru/news/cennosti/ti-tele)**, Söyembikä. Chulpan Galiekhmetova / Чулпан ГАЛИӘХМӘТОВА. Published **1 July 2022, 16:35**.

Read the complete introduction and interview with Marsel Ganiev, through the final answer about online representation of Tatar. He describes learning Tatar as an adult and finding people to speak with while living outside Татарстан.

**Observed wording:** “мәгълүмат эзләдем” (looked for information); “дуслар таптым” (found friends); “ягъни чагылышы” (an explanatory gloss).

**Register:** short interviewer questions and action-led answers sit within a more literary magazine introduction. Unfamiliar borrowed vocabulary receives an explanation. The interviewee is a heritage-language learner; his published answers must not be treated as a grammar authority.

**Use for review:** check whether abstract copy could name the actual action: finding information, talking, learning, or meeting people. Explain a necessary specialist term once. This article is relevant to language and connection across countries, not evidence about Bulachak's services or immigrant identity in general.

## 2. Family skills and village mutual help

**[«Без үз авылыбызга кирәк»](https://syuyumbike.ru/news/tatar-gailse/bez-z-avylybyzga-kirk)**, Söyembikä. Chulpan Galiekhmetova / Чулпан ГАЛИӘХМӘТОВА. Published **12 November 2021, 16:49**.

Read the full Shavaliyev family profile, including intergenerational skills, local communication, volunteering, and the closing conversation. The body ends before its photo gallery.

**Observed wording:** “Бергә уйладык” (thought it through together); “Кешеләргә ярдәм итәсе килә бит.” (wanting to help people); “кемгәдер файдалы” (useful to someone).

**Register:** scene-setting narration alternates with recognizably conversational, personal speech. The dialogue's warmth comes from named people, shared tasks, and specific relationships. The narrator also uses proverbs, praise, and moral judgments characteristic of this magazine feature.

**Use for review:** make community language concrete through helping, teaching, and keeping in touch. I4I's scope should remain broader than commercial networking. Do not transfer the family's experiences, kinship rules, gender roles, or the author's judgments into Bulachak's voice. Conversational particles work in their original exchanges; copying them into every label would sound affected.

## 3. School, families, and a wider community

**[Яктылык](https://syuyumbike.ru/news/millettesh/yaktylyk-mktbe)**, Söyembikä. Gulnur Safiullina / Гөлнур САФИУЛЛИНА. Published **15 December 2021, 20:49**.

Read the entire profile of Samara school director Radik Gazizov, from its opening through the final school statistics. It combines a formal professional introduction, themed personal recollections, and a factual end panel.

**Observed wording:** “Бер-берсе белән танышу, аралашу” (meeting and talking with one another); “кирәк булганда” (when needed); “сорыйм” / “аңлаталар” (I ask / they explain).

**Register:** the official introduction is dense with roles and institutions. The personal sections use concrete verbs, familiar relationships, and short topic headings. The passage about asking children to explain language rules is especially different from bureaucratic service prose.

**Use for review:** distinguish getting acquainted, communicating, asking, and explaining rather than translating all of them into one vague networking term. Short topical headings can support scanning. Keep official names where needed, but do not copy the introductory title stack into a homepage. The speaker's religious, financial, and personal views are his own, not Bulachak principles.

## 4. A language course report with participant and organizer voices

**[Татарстан мәчетләрендә татар телен өйрәнү курслары ачылды](https://intertat.tatar/news/tatarstan-m-chetl-rend-tatar-telen-yr-n-kurslary-achyldy-5694532)**, Intertat. Published **17 October 2019, 14:47**. No individual byline was displayed in the retrieved article; its introduction identifies a Tatar-Inform correspondent.

Read the full report, learner interview, organizer interview, teacher explanation, and final schedule. This is an Intertat publication with explicit Tatar-Inform reporting provenance, not independent corroboration of that outlet. The historical schedule is not current service information.

**Observed wording:** “Ничек итеп Сезгә ярдәм итә алам” (how can I help you); “башлап карамаска” (trying to begin); “мәгълүмат биреп үтик” (introducing information); “юнәлдерелгән” / “күздә тотыла” (formal program/status language).

**Register:** courteous direct speech contrasts with formal course descriptions. The learner's original interview language and any translation process are not stated, so the published answer is evidence of editorial wording, not a raw speech sample.

**Use for review:** use a respectful, clear tone for contact. Retain precise prospective language when describing something planned, but avoid making all branch descriptions read like course administration. This article does not validate any particular teaching method.

# Decisions for the later copy pass

| Surface | Editorial direction suggested by this reading |
| --- | --- |
| Home and branch introductions | Put a person or group and a concrete action in the sentence. Review chains of abstract nouns for clarity rather than treating their presence as proof of an error. |
| I4I | Keep room for language, relatives, friendships, community, and practical help. Business is one possible context, not a replacement for kinship. |
| Contact and navigation | Aim for courteous, concise published language. Do not import intimate forms of address, ornamental proverbs, or interview fillers into controls. |
| Philosophy | Preserve the author's concepts. Familiar actions can explain a specialist idea, but simplification must not remove co-opetition or invent a replacement philosophy. |
| Development status | Distinguish a current action, an intention, and an aspiration. Formal wording may be necessary for precision; these articles do not establish any Bulachak operational claim. |

These are editorial inferences from the reading, not frequency measurements or linguistic rules. Any proposed replacement still needs a sentence-level meaning check against the English source, attention to Tatar grammar and idiom, and review by a fluent Tatar reader. No claim of completed translation validation should be attached to this document.

## Reading and quotation record

- All four bodies were read through their final substantive paragraph or end panel. Menus, reader comments, and related-article teasers were excluded from the language sample.
- The source titles and selected short extracts total fewer than 25 quoted words per article. English glosses are working explanations written for these notes, not published translations.
- Newer Intertat search results that could not be opened in full were excluded. Search snippets alone were not counted as reading evidence.
- The sample is concentrated in the Tatmedia publishing environment, including two pieces by the same author. Its limitations should remain visible when combined with the separate Tatar-Inform notes.
