## Parafencing
short: Fencing from wheelchairs fixed to a frame on the piste. Three weapons, and a distance that is set before the bout and never changed.
avoid: wheelchair fencing (in program communications, where the team name is Parafencing)
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.17, t.18, t.33, t.76.0, t.90.0 and t.96.0
status: official

The rule book's own name for the sport is Wheelchair Fencing. Two fencers sit in [[Fencing Wheelchair|fencing wheelchairs]] fixed to a [[Frame]] on the [[Piste]]. The [[Fencing Distance]] is agreed before the bout and cannot be changed afterwards, and [[Lifting from the Seat]] is forbidden. Competitions are fenced at three weapons: [[Foil]], [[Épée]] and [[Sabre]]. Related term: [[Classification]].

> Can she move her chair back if she keeps getting hit?
> No. The chairs are fixed to the frame, and the distance is set before the bout.

## Weapon
short: Foil, épée or sabre. Each has its own target and its own way of making and judging a touch.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.17, t.21, t.76, t.77, t.90, t.91, t.96 and t.97
status: official

The three weapons are [[Foil]], [[Épée]] and [[Sabre]], and all three are fenced on the same pistes. Each one has its own [[Target Area]] and its own rules for judging a [[Touch]]. The weapon is used with one hand only, and a fencer may not change hands before the end of the bout unless the [[Referee]] gives special permission for an injury to the hand or arm.

## Foil
short: A thrusting weapon: touches are made with the point only. The target is the trunk, and priority decides who scores when both fencers hit.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.76, t.77, t.78 and t.82 to t.89
status: official

Foil scores with the point, on the trunk only (see [[Target Area]]). A hit that lands off the target is not counted, but it stops the phrase and annuls any hit that follows. When both fencers are hit, the [[Referee]] applies [[Priority]], also called right of way, to decide who is awarded the [[Touch]]. Pushing or dragging the point on the piste during the bout is forbidden.

## Épée
short: A thrusting weapon: touches are made with the point only. Everything above the line of the hips is target, and both fencers can score on the same action.
avoid: epee spelled without the accent in the interface
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.38, t.59, t.90, t.91 and t.92
status: official

Épée scores with the point anywhere above a horizontal line drawn between the tops of the hip bones (see [[Target Area]]). When both fencers are hit and the apparatus registers both hits as valid, there is a [[Double Touch]]: a [[Touch]] is scored against each fencer. A [[Conductive Apron]] covers the non-valid areas.

## Sabre
short: A weapon for thrusting and cutting. Everything above the line of the hips is target, and priority decides who scores when both fencers hit.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.96, t.97, t.98 and t.100 to t.106
status: official

Sabre scores with the point, the cutting edge, the flat or the back of the blade, above a horizontal line drawn between the tops of the hip bones (see [[Target Area]]). Hitting with the [[Guard]] is forbidden and such a hit is annulled. A hit on a non-valid part is not registered and does not stop the phrase. When both fencers are hit, the [[Referee]] applies [[Priority]], as in [[Foil]].

## Piste
short: The part of the field of play that is used for fencing. In parafencing the frame that holds the two wheelchairs stands on it.
avoid: strip; strip-side
source: P4P house style; World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.17, t.18 and t.19
status: rule

The piste is where the [[Frame]] sits and where a [[Bout]] is fenced. All three weapons are fenced on the same pistes. In foil and épée there must be a conductive area around the frame, large enough to avoid hits on the ground. Anything that happens beside it is piste-side: piste-side check-ins, piste-side coaching.

> Where do we meet after the pool?
> Piste-side, as soon as the last bout is over.

## Frame
short: The fencing frame used on the piste to fix both wheelchairs in place.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.18, t.33 and t.91
status: official

A fencing frame is used on the [[Piste]] to fix the wheelchairs (see [[Wheelchair Fixing]] and [[Frame Angle]]). It must be arranged so that each fencer can fence with their preferred fencing arm (see [[Sword Arm]]), and every frame used in World Para Fencing competitions must be approved by the World Para Fencing Executive Committee. The [[Fencing Distance]] is agreed before each [[Bout]]. The fencers stay seated: [[Lifting from the Seat]] is forbidden. At épée, a hit registered on the frame is annulled.

## Fencing Distance
short: The gap between the two fencers, set before the bout by an arm and blade measurement and then not changed. The rule book calls it the fencing measure.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.76.0, t.90.0, t.96.0 and t.34
status: official

Both fencers sit upright in the centre of the seat. One bends the fencing arm, forearm vertical, upper arm horizontal, elbow pointing at the opponent. The other, with a straight arm, reaches with the point: to the inner edge of the forearm in [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]], to the outer edge of the elbow in [[Épée]]. Then they swap. If the arms are of unequal length, the fencer with the shorter arm may choose a distance that lies between their own and the opponent's. If there is a dispute, the [[Referee]] decides and that decision is final.

Once both fencers have agreed the distance, it cannot be changed. At épée it is set before the apron is put on. Fencers in [[Category C]] measure to a point 10 centimetres further in. Deliberately unfastening the wheelchair to force a new measurement is an offence (see [[Losing Balance]]).

> Her arm is shorter. Does she have to take his distance?
> No. The fencer with the shorter arm may choose a distance between the two.

## Sword Arm
short: The arm an athlete fences with, left or right. It decides how the frame is arranged.
source: P4P house style; World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.18 and t.21
status: glossary

An athlete's sword arm is the arm they fence with. It matters on the [[Frame]], which must let each fencer use their preferred fencing arm: the rules show one fencing position for two right-handers or two left-handers and another for a right-hander against a left-hander. The weapon is used with one hand only. The other arm is the [[Non-Sword Arm]].

## Touch
short: One valid hit, counted on the score. The rule book's word for it is hit.
avoid: point (for the hit itself)
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.38, t.40, t.47, t.54, t.59 and t.162
status: official

A touch is a hit that is registered by the electrical apparatus and awarded by the [[Referee]]. The referee cannot award a hit that the apparatus has not registered, except for a penalty hit (see [[Red Card]]), and in set cases must annul one that it has registered. Which hits count depends on the weapon's [[Target Area]]. A [[Pool]] bout is fenced to five touches and a [[Direct Elimination]] bout to fifteen. Program term built on this one: [[Video Analysis]].

## Target Area
short: The part of the body where a touch counts. The trunk in foil, everything above the line of the hips in épée and sabre.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.77, t.91 and t.97
status: official

In [[Foil]] the target is the trunk, without the limbs and the head. Its lower limit is a horizontal line across the back joining the tops of the hip bones, then straight lines down to the junction of the lines of the groin. It also includes the lower part of the bib, below a line 1.5 to 2 cm under the chin. In [[Épée]] and [[Sabre]] the target is any part of the body above a horizontal line drawn between the tops of the hip bones. At épée this includes clothing, equipment and any portion of the wheelchair above that line.

At épée a flexible [[Conductive Apron]], earthed to the apparatus, covers the non-valid areas. Protecting the target or putting another part of the body in its place is an offence (see [[Covering the Target]]). A valid hit is a [[Touch]].
