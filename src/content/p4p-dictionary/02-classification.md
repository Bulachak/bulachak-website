## Classification
short: Grouping athletes into sport classes according to how much their impairment affects the basic activities of fencing from a wheelchair.
avoid: grading; handicap rating
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), version February 2023, updated January 2025, art. 1.6, 1.9, 40.1, Glossary and Appendix One section 4
status: official

Classification does two jobs: it defines who is eligible to compete, and it groups athletes so that the impact of impairment is kept small and sporting excellence decides the result. Through [[Athlete Evaluation]] each athlete is allocated a [[Sport Class]] and a [[Sport Class Status]]. At competitions the sport classes are combined into [[Category A]], [[Category B]] and [[Category C]]. An athlete may fence internationally only with a sport class (other than [[Not Eligible]]) and a status. World Para Fencing publishes both on its Classification Master List.

> Is she confirmed yet?
> Not yet. Her status is Review, so she is evaluated again before her next international competition.

## Category A
short: The competition category for Sport Classes 3 and 4: athletes with good sitting balance and a normal fencing arm.
avoid: "Athlete A" as a label for a hypothetical fencer; "Camp Group A"
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), Appendix One, 4.4, 4.5 and Categories; Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.4.2, o.97 and o.104; label wording is P4P house style
status: official

Category A combines two [[Sport Class|sport classes]]. Class 3 is good sitting balance without the support of the legs, and Class 4 is good sitting balance with the support of the lower limbs. Both have a normal [[Sword Arm|fencing arm]]. A Category A fencer cannot enter a [[Category B]] or [[Category C]] [[Event]]. Hypothetical fencers are never named with single letters, because the letter reads as a category.

## Category B
short: The competition category for Sport Class 2: athletes with fair sitting balance and a normal fencing arm.
avoid: "Athlete B" as a label for a hypothetical fencer; "Camp Group B"
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), Appendix One, 4.3 and Categories; Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.4.2, o.97 and o.104; label wording is P4P house style
status: official

Category B holds one [[Sport Class]], Class 2. A Category B fencer may choose to fence in a [[Category A]] [[Event]], but not in a [[Category C]] one (see [[Fencing in a Higher Category]]). Every team needs Category B coverage: see [[Team Category Composition]]. See [[Category A]] for the labelling rule.

> Who fences the B position in the sabre team?
> It has to be a Category B fencer, or a Category C fencer fencing up.

## Category C
short: The competition category for Sport Classes 1A and 1B: athletes without sitting balance and with an affected fencing arm.
avoid: "Athlete C" as a label for a hypothetical fencer
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), Appendix One, 4.1, 4.2 and Categories; Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.4.1, o.4.2, o.36, o.41, o.42 and o.43
status: official

In both [[Sport Class|sport classes]] of Category C the weapon is fixed to the hand. Senior World Championships and [[Zonal Championships]] list Category C individual [[Event|events]] in [[Foil]] and [[Épée]], and at a [[World Cup]] they are optional. The Paralympic Games program lists individual events in [[Category A]] and [[Category B]] only. A Category C fencer may choose to fence in a Category A or Category B event, or in a [[Team Event]]: see [[Fencing in a Higher Category]].

## Sport Class
short: The class an athlete is placed in after evaluation. Wheelchair fencing has five: 1A, 1B, 2, 3 and 4.
avoid: disability class; using "category" and "sport class" as the same thing
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 9.1, 9.1.3 and Appendix One section 4 (4.1 to 4.5 and Categories)
status: official

A sport class groups athletes by the impact of an [[Eligible Impairment]] on the tasks and activities fundamental to fencing. The rules describe the five classes by sitting balance and by the function of the [[Sword Arm|fencing arm]], from 1A to 4. The class is allocated by a [[Classification Panel]], and it always comes with a [[Sport Class Status]]. For competition the classes are combined: 3 and 4 form [[Category A]], 2 is [[Category B]], 1A and 1B form [[Category C]].

## Sport Class Status
short: The label beside a sport class that says whether the athlete must be evaluated again: New, Review, Review with a Fixed Review Date, or Confirmed.
avoid: provisional; permanent (for Confirmed)
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 15.1 to 15.7, 21.4.1 and 30.7
status: official

New (N) is the status before an athlete's first evaluation. Review (R) means the [[Classification Panel]] expects further evaluation, and the athlete completes [[Athlete Evaluation]] before the next international competition unless World Para Fencing says otherwise. Review with a Fixed Review Date (FRD) means no new evaluation is needed before a set date. Confirmed (C) means the panel is satisfied that the impairment and the athlete's ability to perform the tasks of the sport are and will remain stable. An accepted [[Protest]], an accepted Medical Review Request or a change in the class criteria can move an athlete back to Review.

> She is Class 2 Confirmed. Does she see the classifiers at the World Cup?
> No. Confirmed means no further evaluation, unless there is a protest or a medical review.

## Athlete Evaluation
short: The process that allocates an athlete a sport class and a sport class status.
avoid: classification exam; medical
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 6.1, 6.2, 12.4, 12.4.1, 13.3, 16.1 and Appendix One section 3 (including 3.10)
status: official

Athlete evaluation has three steps: checking that the athlete has an [[Eligible Impairment]], checking the [[Minimum Impairment Criteria]], and allocating a [[Sport Class]] with a [[Sport Class Status]]. The second and third steps happen in an evaluation session with a [[Classification Panel]], in a controlled, non-competitive setting. It combines a physical assessment (the [[Bench Test]] and the [[Wheelchair Functional Tests]]) with a technical assessment of fencing moves such as the on guard position, the lunge, the recovery and the backward lean. Low fitness, poor technique and aging must not affect the class. The athlete brings identification and their own [[Fencing Wheelchair]], weapons, straps and gloves.

## Eligible Impairment
short: An impairment the rules accept as a condition for competing. It must be permanent.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 7.1 to 7.5, Glossary (Permanent) and Appendix One, 1.1 to 1.3
status: official

Wheelchair fencing lists six eligible impairment types: impaired muscle power, impaired passive range of movement, limb deficiency and limb length difference, hypertonia, ataxia and athetosis. The athlete's national body submits a Medical Diagnostics Form at least six weeks before the [[Classification]]. An athlete without an eligible impairment is allocated [[Not Eligible]]. Having an eligible impairment is not enough by itself: it must also meet the [[Minimum Impairment Criteria]].

## Minimum Impairment Criteria
short: The least degree of eligible impairment an athlete must show to compete in wheelchair fencing. Often shortened to MIC.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 8.1 to 8.6 and Appendix One, 2.1 and 2.3.2
status: official

The criteria make sure that an [[Eligible Impairment]] really affects the tasks and activities fundamental to the sport. Each impairment type has its own threshold: for impaired muscle power it is a loss of at least 20 points in one lower limb, or at least 25 points across both, and for limb length difference it is 7 cm or more between the legs. A [[Classification Panel]] checks the criteria during [[Athlete Evaluation]], without taking adaptive equipment into account. An athlete who does not meet them is allocated [[Not Eligible]].

## Classification Panel
short: The group of classifiers that evaluates an athlete and allocates the sport class and status.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 3.13, 11.1 to 11.3, 12.1, 12.2 and 13.4.2
status: official

A panel has at least two certified [[Classifier|classifiers]]. In exceptional circumstances it may be one classifier who holds a medical qualification, and that panel can only give the [[Sport Class Status]] Review. The panel checks the [[Minimum Impairment Criteria]], assesses how far the athlete can perform the tasks of the sport, and may ask for [[Observation in Competition]]. It then allocates a [[Sport Class]] and a status, or records Classification Not Completed. Sessions are held in English unless World Para Fencing decides otherwise.

## Classifier
short: An official certified by World Para Fencing to evaluate athletes as a member of a classification panel.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 3.2, 3.7, 3.8, 3.10, 4.3.5 and 5.2
status: official

A classifier is either a certified health professional, such as a physician or physiotherapist, or a person with an extensive coaching background in wheelchair fencing or an accepted academic qualification. Athletes who are still competing may not train as classifiers. A Chief Classifier runs classification at one competition, and the Head of Classification directs it for World Para Fencing as a whole. All of them work under the Classifier Code of Conduct and sit on a [[Classification Panel]] to carry out [[Athlete Evaluation]].

## Bench Test
short: The part of the physical assessment done off the wheelchair: muscle strength and range of movement, scored from 0 to 5.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), Appendix One, 3.4.1 table, 3.5, 3.6.1 and 3.6.2
status: official

The athlete transfers onto a plinth and the [[Classifier|classifiers]] test muscle strength against manual resistance on the Daniels and Worthingham scale, from 0 (no voluntary contraction) to 5 (normal strength). Upper limbs, lower limbs and trunk are tested, including the [[Sword Arm|fencing arm]] and the other arm. The trunk bench test has five movements, for a total out of 25. Together with the [[Wheelchair Functional Tests]] it gives the scores that place an athlete in a [[Sport Class]].

## Wheelchair Functional Tests
short: Six trunk tests done seated in the wheelchair, with and without a weapon. Each is scored from 0 to 3.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), Appendix One, 3.4, 3.4.1 and section 4
status: official

The six tests are upper extension, side balance, lumbar extension, side balance with weapon, 45 degree rotated extension and 45 degree hold. They repeat fencing movements such as the lunge and the fast return of the trunk. A score of 0 means the movement cannot be done and 3 means normal execution, for a total out of 18. Added to the trunk [[Bench Test]] the trunk total is out of 43. Each [[Sport Class]] is described by a range of these scores.

> What did the panel ask her to do in the chair?
> The six functional tests. Leaning, holding and coming back up, some of them with the weapon in hand.

## Observation in Competition
short: The classification panel watching an athlete fence in competition before it settles the sport class and status.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 14.1 to 14.6.1 and Appendix One, 3.3 and 3.3.1
status: official

A [[Classification Panel]] may require observation before it gives a final [[Sport Class]]. The athlete enters the [[Competition]] with the class allocated after the first parts of the evaluation and carries the tracking code Observation Assessment (OA) in place of a [[Sport Class Status]]. Observation takes place at First Appearance, the first time the athlete competes in that class at the competition. The classifiers watch the lunge, the recovery, trunk range of motion, accuracy and coordination, and score each item from 0 to 3 on an observation card. Any change that follows takes effect immediately.

## Not Eligible
short: The outcome, written NE, for an athlete who has no eligible impairment or does not meet the minimum impairment criteria. The athlete cannot compete in the sport.
avoid: failed classification; not disabled enough
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 8.4, 9.1.1, 17.1 to 17.9 and 18.2
status: official

There are two routes to Not Eligible. If World Para Fencing finds no [[Eligible Impairment]], the athlete does not attend an evaluation session and the decision is not reviewed by a second panel. If a [[Classification Panel]] finds that the [[Minimum Impairment Criteria]] are not met, a second panel must review the athlete as soon as practicable, and the athlete does not compete in the meantime. Not Eligible cannot be the subject of a [[Protest]]. The rules state that it does not question the presence of a genuine impairment: it is only a ruling on eligibility for this sport.

## Protest
short: A reasoned objection to an athlete's sport class, made by a national body, a National Paralympic Committee or World Para Fencing.
avoid: appeal (a different procedure)
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 18.1, 19, 20.1 to 20.3, 21.1 to 21.4, 24.3 and 24.7
status: official

A protest can challenge a [[Sport Class]] only, never a [[Sport Class Status]]. A national protest concerns an athlete under that body's own jurisdiction. It is filed on a Protest Form with a fee, within one hour of the result being published at the competition, or within fifteen minutes when the result follows [[Observation in Competition]]. If the Chief Classifier accepts it, the class stays as it is, the status becomes Review, and a Protest Panel of different [[Classifier|classifiers]] holds a new evaluation session. The decision of the Protest Panel is final.

## Appeal
short: A formal objection to how classification procedures were carried out, not to the sport class itself.
avoid: protest (a different procedure)
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 41.1, 42.1, 43.1 to 43.3 and Glossary (BAC)
status: official

Only a national body or a National Paralympic Committee can appeal. The ground is a procedural error in allocating a [[Sport Class]] or [[Sport Class Status]] that left the athlete with the wrong one. Appeals are heard by the Board of Appeal of Classification of the International Paralympic Committee, under its own bylaws. An objection to the class itself is a [[Protest]].

## Intentional Misrepresentation
short: Deliberately misleading classifiers about one's skills, abilities or impairment. It is a disciplinary offence.
source: World Para Fencing Classification Rules (Book 4), art. 31.1 to 31.6
status: official

The offence can be committed by act or by omission, during [[Athlete Evaluation]] or at any time after a [[Sport Class]] has been allocated. Helping an athlete to do it, or covering it up, is also an offence for athletes and support personnel. After a hearing held by World Para Fencing, the consequences include disqualification from the competition, a [[Not Eligible]] allocation or a suspension for one to four years, and publication of the name. A second offence brings four years to life.
