## Fencing Wheelchair
short: The chair an athlete fences from. It counts as fencing equipment and is checked before use at every tournament.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.1 to m.25.9.4 and m.37.1
status: official

The wheelchair is part of the fencer's equipment, so it goes through [[Weapon Control]] before each tournament and receives a [[Control Mark]]. Its dimensions are checked with gauges: the [[Backrest]], the [[Seating Platform]], the [[Cushion]], the [[Side Guard]] and the [[Footrest]] each have their own limits. A modification that takes the chair outside those limits needs the approval of both the SEMI Commission and the classifiers (see [[Classification]]). The fencer is responsible for the chair at the moment of arriving on the [[Piste]].

> Does my chair go to control, or only the weapons?
> The chair too. It is equipment, and it is checked before every tournament.

## Backrest
short: The rigid back of the fencing wheelchair. It stands at least 15 cm above the seat or cushion, at 90 degrees to the horizontal.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.5 and m.25.9.6
status: official

The back of the [[Fencing Wheelchair]] must be at least 15 cm high, measured from the seat or from the [[Cushion]] with the fencer sitting on it, and must stand at 90 degrees to the horizontal (+2 degrees). It may be padded, but the material must be rigid (wood, acrylic or metal, for example) and must not compress below 15 cm. On the fencer's side it can be any colour that contrasts with the uniform. When a fencer has a physical need for a different back, the wheelchair controller and a classifier decide. The back also carries the [[Back Centre Line]].

## Back Centre Line
short: A vertical line 10 cm long and 2.5 cm wide, marked on the centre of the wheelchair back and on the back of the jacket.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.7, m.25.4.e, m.28.9 and m.34.6
status: official

On the [[Backrest]] the line sits on the outer side, in the centre, with its top level with the top edge of the back, in a colour that contrasts with the back. It must be permanent (stitched, painted or printed). Tape and hook and loop tape are not accepted, and the line must not shift left or right when the upholstery is rotated. If it is found to have moved by more than 1.25 cm during the competition, that is treated as an offence of antisporting behaviour. The same size of line, in dark blue and 5 cm above the lower edge, goes on the back of the épée jacket and of the foil and sabre [[Conductive Jacket]].

## Seating Platform
short: The surface the fencer sits on. Its whole area may be no higher than 53 cm from the floor.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.8 to m.25.9.10, m.25.9.21 and m.25.9.23
status: official

The full area of the seating platform has a maximum height of 53 cm from the floor, measured at all four corners with all tyres fully inflated. The four values may differ by no more than 10 mm. For [[Category A]] fencers only, the platform must also be parallel to the floor. The seat may not be more than 6 cm wider than the seated fencer's hips, which is 3 cm from the hips when the fencer sits in the centre. Wheels of any size are allowed as long as they do not lift the platform above 53 cm. A [[Cushion]] may be placed on top.

> We changed to bigger wheels last month. Is that a problem?
> Only if the platform now measures more than 53 cm. Pump the tyres up fully and measure all four corners.

## Cushion
short: An optional pad on the wheelchair seat. It may be up to 10 cm thick, even in thickness, and the same size as the seat.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.11
status: official

A cushion is not required. When one is used on the [[Seating Platform]] it must have the same dimensions as the seat, an even thickness of no more than 10 cm, and no wedge shape. The wheelchair controller must be able to bend it corner to corner. Its upper side can be any colour that contrasts with the fencer's uniform. A medical cushion needs the authorisation of the classifier (see [[Classification]]).

## Side Guard
short: A rigid, clear panel on the non-fencing-arm side of the wheelchair. No side guard is allowed on the fencing arm side.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.12 and m.25.9.13
status: official

The side guard sits on the side opposite the [[Sword Arm]]. It must be at least 10 cm high, measured from the seat or the [[Cushion]] with the fencer seated, and at least 20 cm long starting from the back of the chair. It must be securely fixed and rigid enough to hold an angle of 90 degrees to the [[Backrest]]. Tape is not accepted as a fixing. The material should be clear, transparent and uncoloured, and if a reinforcement is fitted, a transparent area of at least 18 cm by 9 cm must remain.

## Strapping
short: Belts or straps that hold the fencer, the legs or the unarmed hand to the wheelchair. It is allowed, with limits.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.14 to m.25.9.18
status: official

A fencer may be strapped to the [[Fencing Wheelchair]]. A strap that holds the legs steady must not be elastic. A strap on the unarmed hand must not be elastic or loose and should bind the hand to the grab handle, unless a classifier has approved an exception. The grab handle itself is optional: if fitted, it is mounted vertically above the [[Side Guard]], and its mounting may lead away from the fencer but never toward the fencer. No strapping may cover the valid [[Target Area]].

## Footrest
short: The platform for the feet on the fencing wheelchair. One must be fitted, and it may be no more than 40 cm deep.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.9.20
status: official

A footrest must be fitted to the [[Fencing Wheelchair]]. It must not be wider than the chair between the two vertical struts of the wheelchair frame, and its depth is limited to 40 cm. For a fencer with one foot, a vertical strut no more than 5 cm high may be fitted across the footrest to stop the foot from sliding. If a fencer cannot use a footrest, a classifier must confirm and document that (see [[Classification]]).

## Spoke Protectors
short: Covers fitted over the spokes of both rear wheels of the fencing wheelchair. They are required.
avoid: wheel covers
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.3.f, m.25.3.g, m.25.9.2 and m.25.9.25
status: official

Both rear wheels of the [[Fencing Wheelchair]] must carry spoke protectors. At the Paralympic Games, World Championships, Zonal Championships, Zonal Games and World Cups, they must show the design approved by World Para Fencing. The design has to be approved by the WPF Executive Committee at least 30 days before its first use. No athlete name is allowed on them. A country name or national code is optional, in capital letters no more than 10 cm high. The [[Control Mark]] for the wheels is not placed on the spoke protectors.

## Wheelchair Fixing
short: How the frame holds the chair: every wheel is secured so that distance, angle and width cannot change during the bout.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.61 and m.25.9.24
status: official

The [[Frame]] fixes the [[Fencing Wheelchair]] in a stable position by securing all of its wheels. The front wheels should touch the front stop, and all wheels should rest on the base of the frame. The frame adjusts for different chair widths, for left-handed fencers and for the [[Fencing Distance]], and once set, nothing may move during the [[Bout]]. If a wheelchair does not fit the frame, a replacement chair that fits must be used. The camber of the wheels must fit the official frame and should be no more than 5 degrees from vertical.

> My chair is wider than most. What if it does not fit?
> The frame adjusts for width. If it still does not fit, the rules say a replacement chair that fits must be used.

## Frame Angle
short: The angle at which the frame holds each wheelchair: 110 degrees, give or take 5, to the central line.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.61
status: official

The [[Frame]] fixes both wheelchairs at an angle of 110 degrees (plus or minus 5 degrees). The angle is measured against a theoretical central line drawn between the ends of the front wheel stops on the two platforms. The frame must be built so that this angle cannot change during the [[Bout]] (see [[Wheelchair Fixing]]).

## Conductive Apron
short: The conductive cover worn in épée over the legs and the lower chair, so hits below the target do not register.
avoid: skirt; blanket
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.62, m.31.3, m.38.1 and m.44.8
status: official

The apron is part of the fencer's own equipment in [[Épée]] and is tested before every official World Para Fencing competition. It is made of the same lamé material as the [[Conductive Jacket]] and covers the area outside the [[Target Area]]. In the on guard position it must touch the [[Piste]], so that no valid hit can land on the feet, the lower parts of the wheelchair or the fencing platform. It is earthed by a ground cable, which the organisers provide. The three-letter nationality code must be printed on it in dark blue capitals 8 cm to 10 cm high, placed 10 cm to 20 cm above the piste. No advertising and no fencer's name are allowed on it.

> The apron keeps riding up when I lean forward.
> Fix it so it stays down. On guard it has to touch the piste, and it must not come loose during the bout.

## Conductive Jacket
short: The over-jacket of lamé material worn in foil and sabre. It covers the valid target so that touches there register.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.28, m.34 and m.42.2.g
status: official

In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]] the conductive jacket is worn over the protective jacket (see [[Protective Clothing]]) and must cover the whole valid [[Target Area]] when the fencer sits on guard. In sabre it covers the body above the line joining the tops of the hip bones, and the arms as far as the wrists. The resistance between any two points of the lamé must not exceed 5 ohms, and a jacket with holes or patches of oxidation is not allowed. The collar is at least 3 cm high, and a conductive flap of 2 cm by 3 cm below the collar takes the clip of the wire from the [[Mask]]. Control stamps the jacket, but the referee makes the final decision on whether it covers the target and may be used.

## Mask
short: Head protection with a wire mesh front and a cloth bib. The bib must resist 1600 newtons.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.7, m.27, m.30 and m.32
status: official

The mesh has openings of at most 2.1 mm and wires at least 1 mm in diameter, and the mask has two different safety systems at the rear. The bib is made of cloth resistant to 1600 newtons. In [[Foil]] the mesh is insulated and the part of the bib below a line 1.5 cm to 2 cm under the chin is conductive. In [[Sabre]] the mesh, bib and trim conduct. In [[Épée]] the mask carries no conductive material, and the bib reaches below the collar bones. In foil and sabre a white or clear wire, 30 cm to 40 cm long and not coiled, connects the mask to the [[Conductive Jacket]] with two crocodile clips.

> Can I bring my coiled mask wire for sabre?
> No. A coiled mask wire is not allowed. It has to be white or clear, and 30 to 40 cm long.

## Glove
short: Worn on the sword hand. Its gauntlet must cover about half the forearm so that a blade cannot enter the sleeve.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.6, m.26 and m.33
status: official

At all three weapons the gauntlet of the glove fully covers about half the forearm of the [[Sword Arm]]. There must be no hole in the hand of the glove, not even for the [[Body Wire]]. In [[Foil]] the glove may be slightly padded. In [[Sabre]] the glove material has a protection level of 800 newtons on the areas shown in the rule's diagram, with seams of at least 200 newtons and a cuff of 350 newtons. The sabre glove also carries conductive material over the gauntlet, turned at least 5 cm into the inside, and the FIE quality label inside (see [[Homologation]]).

## Protective Clothing
short: The jacket, under-plastron and breeches or trousers. The cloth must resist a pressure of 800 newtons.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.2 to m.25.5 and m.25.8
status: official

Jackets, under-plastrons, breeches and trousers must be made entirely of cloth able to resist 800 newtons, and an under-plastron resistant to 800 newtons is worn under the jacket. Clothing must be clean, in good condition and carry the FIE label (see [[Homologation]]). It may be any colour apart from black. When the fencer sits on guard, the lower edge of the jacket overlaps the breeches by at least 10 cm. Breeches fasten below the knees and are worn with matching socks that reach up to them. With trousers, socks protect the ankles and any exposed part of the legs. Leg prostheses are removed during a [[Bout]]. A [[Chest Protector]] is worn under the clothing.

## Chest Protector
short: A rigid protector for the chest. Women must wear one at all three weapons. For men it is optional.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.4.c and m.25.4.d
status: official

The chest protector is made of metal or another rigid material. In [[Foil]] it is worn below the protective plastron (see [[Protective Clothing]]), and its whole outer side must be covered with a soft material such as E.V.A., 4 mm thick, bearing the FIE SEMI technical mark at the centre of the upper edge. Exceptions for medical needs, such as a surgical brace, are approved by World Para Fencing international classifiers before the competition (see [[Classification]]). From 1 October 2026, a brace with a hard surface in the [[Target Area]] must have that whole area covered with the same kind of soft material.

## Body Wire
short: The fencer's own cable. It connects the socket inside the guard of the weapon to the spool cable at the fencer's back.
avoid: body cord
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.29, m.31, m.35, m.38.1 and m.55.2
status: official

The body wire is personal equipment. It has a plug of transparent material at each end, and each of its conductive wires may have a resistance of no more than 1 ohm. If the weapon has no security device to stop the plug from coming out of the [[Guard]], the device must be on the plug of the body wire. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]] a crocodile clip joins the wire to the back of the [[Conductive Jacket]] on the [[Sword Arm]] side, and sabre uses the foil body wire. In [[Épée]] an extra wire of at least 50 cm with a crocodile clip may be fitted to ensure contact with the [[Conductive Apron]]. A fencer may present three body wires per weapon at [[Weapon Control]].

## Blade
short: The flexible steel part of the weapon. Official World Para Fencing tournaments require standard size 5 blades in maraging steel.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.1 to m.3, m.8 and m.16
status: official

Every [[Weapon]] has a flexible steel blade that ends in a button at the front and in the tang at the rear, inside the handle. Maraging steel is required at all three weapons. Size 5 means 90 cm for [[Foil]] and [[Épée]] blades (minimum 89 cm) and 88 cm for [[Sabre]] blades (minimum 87 cm). Blade length is measured from the tip to the front face of the [[Guard]]. A foil or épée blade should be as straight as possible, and any curve must be uniform and less than 1 cm. Grinding, filing or otherwise treating the blade between the guard and the tip is forbidden. Other weapon limits are under [[Weapon Dimensions]].

## Guard
short: The metal shell between the blade and the handle. It protects the sword hand and holds the socket for the body wire.
avoid: bell
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.2, m.5, m.9, m.17 and m.24
status: official

The guard is fixed with its convex face toward the front. That face must be smooth, not too shiny, without a raised rim, and must not hold or catch the opponent's point. A [[Foil]] guard has a diameter between 9.5 cm and 12 cm, with the [[Blade]] through its centre. An [[Épée]] guard must pass through a cylindrical gauge 13.5 cm in diameter and is 3 cm to 5.5 cm deep. A [[Sabre]] guard must pass through a rectangular gauge of 15 cm by 14 cm. In foil and épée the guard holds padding less than 2 cm thick. The socket inside takes the [[Body Wire]]. The weapon's electrical resistance may be no more than 2 ohms in foil and épée and 1 ohm in sabre.

## Weapon Point
short: The spring-loaded tip of a foil or épée, called the pointe d'arret in the rules. Pressing it registers the hit.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.5.5, m.11, m.13, m.19 and m.23.2
status: official

In [[Foil]] the point must take a pressure of more than 500 g before a hit registers, and its total travel may be no more than 1 mm. In [[Épée]] the pressure is more than 750 g, the lighting stroke is greater than 1 mm, and the total travel checked on the [[Piste]] is greater than 1.5 mm. The spring must lift the test weight. Only traditional or homologated points are accepted at [[Weapon Control]] (see [[Homologation]]), and they must be clean. The foil [[Blade]] is insulated for 15 cm from the button, but foils are presented at control with the first 15 cm free of adhesive tape. A [[Sabre]] has no spring point: the end of its blade is folded over or formed into a button.

> The weight dropped on my épée at the piste.
> Then it fails. The spring has to lift the 750 gram weight.

## Weapon Dimensions
short: The length and weight limits for a complete weapon. Foil and épée: 110 cm at most. Sabre: 105 cm at most.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.4, m.6, m.7, m.14, m.15, m.21 and m.22
status: official

A [[Foil]] ready for use weighs less than 500 g and is at most 110 cm long. An [[Épée]] weighs less than 770 g and is at most 110 cm long. A [[Sabre]] weighs less than 500 g and is at most 105 cm long. The handle is at most 20 cm long in foil and épée and 17 cm in sabre, and it must pass through the same gauge as the [[Guard]]. An orthopaedic handle must fix one position only for the hand. The limits for the [[Blade]] are listed separately.

## Weapon Control
short: The check of a fencer's weapons, clothing and wheelchair before a competition, done at the Weapon Checking Centre.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.36, m.37, m.38, m.40 and m.43
status: official

Fencers present the equipment they intend to use at the Weapon Checking Centre at the time given in the timetable. Per weapon fenced, the limit is four weapons, three [[Body Wire|body wires]], two [[Conductive Jacket|conductive jackets]], two [[Mask|masks]], three mask wires and two [[Glove|gloves]]. The limit also covers two [[Conductive Apron|conductive aprons]], two jackets, two pairs of breeches or trousers, two under-plastrons and, for foil, two [[Chest Protector|chest protectors]]. Equipment presented after 5 p.m. on the day before the event may be refused. Items that pass receive a [[Control Mark]]. Passing control does not remove the fencer's responsibility: fencers answer for their equipment when they arrive on the [[Piste]].

> When do we hand the bags in?
> At the time in the timetable. Anything handed in after 5 p.m. the day before the event may be refused.

## Control Mark
short: The mark put on each item that has passed weapon control. Equipment without it may not be used.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.41, m.42.2 and m.25.9.2
status: official

Checked items are distinctively marked, and a fencer who uses equipment without the mark faces penalties. Weapons are marked on the [[Guard]], the [[Blade]] and the point with a special ink or paint, or by another method. A [[Conductive Jacket]] gets a stamp showing that its resistance has been checked. A [[Fencing Wheelchair]] that passes gets six identical numbered labels: on the back, the cushion, the frame, the armrest and both large wheels. Only the control marker of the current competition may appear on the chair. The mark comes from [[Weapon Control]].

> There is still a label from the last World Cup on my chair.
> Take it off. Only the marker for this competition may be on the chair.

## Rejected Equipment
short: Equipment that fails weapon control. It is not marked and cannot be used until it is repaired and checked again.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.7, m.28.5, m.36.3, m.38.5, m.39 and m.41.2
status: official

When a weapon fails the first check at [[Weapon Control]], a form naming the fault is attached, and a rejected weapon goes through the entire cycle again. Repairs can be made in the repair workshop, but repaired items are tested only after the first round of checks on the other fencers' equipment is finished. A [[Mask]] that does not meet the safety requirements is made visibly unusable. An unusable [[Conductive Jacket]] is marked with very visible coloured paint. Delegates of the SEMI Commission may seize any item for examination at any time. If equipment appears to be assembled so that the fencer can control the registering of hits, a penalty may be required against the person who submitted it.

## Homologation
short: Official certification that a type of fencing equipment meets the rules. Homologated items carry a label.
source: World Para Fencing Material Rules, January 2026, m.25.2, m.33.1, m.37.2, m.61 and Annexe to the Material Rules
status: official

Fencing material used in official World Para Fencing competitions and carrying the FIE logo must have a certificate of homologation. Homologation is required for blades, points, masks, gloves and protective clothing (jackets, breeches and under-plastrons), and also for scoring apparatus, pistes and wheelchair fencing frames. Material specific to the sport, such as the [[Frame]], is homologated by the World Para Fencing SEMI Commission, and every approved frame carries a plate with its date of homologation, manufacturer and model. A [[Blade]], a [[Mask]] and [[Protective Clothing]] must carry the label of guarantee (see [[Weapon Control]]).
