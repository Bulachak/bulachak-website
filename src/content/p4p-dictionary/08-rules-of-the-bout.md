## Referee
short: The official who directs the bout, awards the touches, penalises faults and keeps order.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.47, t.48, t.59, t.137 and t.172
status: official

Every [[Bout]] at an official World Para Fencing competition is directed by a referee who holds a World Para Fencing refereeing licence for the year. Before each bout the referee checks the weapons, wheelchairs, clothing and equipment. The referee gives the commands [[On Guard|En garde]], [[Allez]] and [[Halt]], decides every [[Touch]], and shows the [[Yellow Card]], [[Red Card]] or [[Black Card]]. Two judges may assist by watching for [[Lifting from the Seat|lifting]], use of the [[Non-Sword Arm]], [[Covering the Target|covering]] and hits on the floor at épée. Judges are required in the final of four of an individual event and in the final of a team event.

A referee's decision on a point of fact, such as the validity or priority of a touch, cannot be appealed, except through [[Video Refereeing]].

## Fencer's Salute
short: The gesture of civility each fencer makes to the opponent, the referee and the spectators, before the bout and after the final touch.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.1, t.122 and t.170
status: official

To salute, the two fencers face each other, sit still in their fencing wheelchairs, hold the [[Mask]] under the [[Non-Sword Arm|non-sword arm]], lift the guard to the chin and then lower the blade to the ground. They salute before each [[Bout]]. After the final [[Touch]] they sit still while the [[Referee]] decides, then salute again, and only then has the bout ended. Refusing to salute is an offence of the fourth group, punished with a [[Black Card]].

## On Guard
short: The still, upright starting position each fencer takes before the referee says "Allez!". Also the referee's order to take it, "En garde!".
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.22 and t.32
status: official

On guard, a fencer sits upright in the centre of the width of the wheelchair seat with both feet on the [[Footrest]]. The fencer may rotate the upper torso toward the opponent, but the hips stay facing directly forward. The sword arms may not be extended, the blades may not touch, and neither point may reach beyond the front of the opponent's guard. Both fencers stay completely still until [[Allez]]. A Classifier can grant an exemption from the seat and footrest requirements (see [[Classification]]).

Deliberately changing the sitting position before "Allez!" to gain an advantage is an offence of the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]). During the bout the soles of the feet must stay on the footrest and may not touch other equipment or the floor. A touch scored by a fencer who breaks this rule is annulled.

> She keeps shifting in the seat before the command.
> If the referee sees it as deliberate, that is a first group offence. Still until "Allez!".

## Allez
short: The referee's command that starts the fencing. Nothing may move before it.
avoid: Play (the rule book's English rendering, not said on the piste); Fence
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.22, t.23, t.31 and t.55; the spoken French form is P4P house style, as referees say it in competition
status: official

The [[Referee]] says "En garde!", asks "Prêts?", and on a yes, or when nobody says no, starts the fencing with "Allez!". The English text of the rule book renders these commands as "On guard!", "Are you ready?" and "Play!", but on the piste they are spoken in French. Any movement made or begun before "Allez!" is not allowed and is an offence of the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]). A touch that comes from an action started before "Allez!" is disregarded. The time of a bout runs from each "Allez!" to the next [[Halt]] (see [[Duration of the Bout]]). The starting position is described under [[On Guard]].

## Halt
short: The referee's command that stops the fencing. No new action may start after it.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.23, t.34, t.44 and t.58
status: official

The [[Bout]] stops on the word "Halt!" (in French, "Halte!"). Only a movement that had begun before the order remains valid. Everything after it does not count. If a fencer stops before "Halt!" and is hit, the [[Touch]] is valid. The [[Referee]] also calls "Halt!" when the fencing is dangerous, confused or against the rules, when a fencer is disarmed, and when a fencer is [[Losing Balance|losing balance]]. When the fencing time runs out, the audible signal of the clock stops the bout. Fencing restarts with [[Allez]].

## Fencing Time
short: The time needed to perform one simple fencing action. A unit for judging actions, not minutes on the clock.
avoid: bout time; clock time
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.8, t.37, t.88, t.89, t.103 and t.106
status: official

The [[Referee]] uses fencing time to judge who is hit when both fencers land. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]], a stop hit against a compound [[Attack]] is valid only if it arrives one period of fencing time before the attacker's final movement (see [[Counter-Attack]]). The minutes of a bout are a different measure: see [[Duration of the Bout]].

## Attack
short: The first offensive action: the fencer extends the arm and keeps threatening the opponent's target.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.9, t.10, t.83 and t.101
status: official

An attack is simple when it is made in one movement, either direct (in the same line) or indirect (in another line). It is compound when it is made in several movements. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]], an attack that is correctly executed must be parried or completely avoided, which is the basis of [[Priority]]. At foil, actions and feints made with a bent arm are counted as preparations, not attacks. The answers to an attack are the [[Parry]] and the [[Counter-Attack]].

## Parry
short: The defensive action made with the weapon to stop an offensive action from arriving.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.9, t.14, t.21, t.86 and t.105
status: official

With all three weapons, defence must be made with the weapon only. A parry is simple and direct when it is made in the same line as the [[Attack]], and circular when it is made in the opposite line. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]] the parry gives the right to [[Riposte]]. At sabre, a parry is properly made when, before the attack is completed, it closes the line in which the attack is to finish. The attack is then judged as parried even if the flexible tip of the blade still touches the target.

## Riposte
short: The offensive action made by the fencer who has just parried the attack.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.9, t.11, t.86 and t.105
status: official

A riposte follows a [[Parry]] and may be immediate or delayed, simple or compound. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]], a riposte annuls any further action by the attacker only if it is made immediately, without indecision or delay (see [[Priority]]). The counter-riposte is the offensive action of the fencer who has parried the riposte.

> Both lamps came on. Why was it her touch?
> She parried and riposted immediately. The parry gave her the right to riposte.

## Counter-Attack
short: An offensive or offensive-defensive action made during the opponent's own offensive action. The stop hit is the main one.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.12, t.88, t.89, t.103 and t.106
status: official

A stop hit is a counter-attack made into an [[Attack]]. A stop hit with opposition also closes the line in which the attack will finish. In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]], a stop hit against a compound attack is valid only when it arrives before the attacker begins the final movement, that is, one period of [[Fencing Time]] ahead. Against a simple attack, with no fencing time between the two hits, the fencer who made the stop hit is the one counted as hit (see [[Priority]]).

## Priority
short: The convention in foil and sabre that decides which fencer is awarded the touch when both are hit. Often called right of way.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.59, t.82 to t.89 and t.100 to t.106
status: official

In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]] the [[Referee]] alone decides the validity or priority of a [[Touch]] by analysing the last fencing phrase. A correctly executed [[Attack]] must be parried or completely avoided. A [[Parry]] gives the right to [[Riposte]]. If the attacker tries to find the opponent's blade and misses it, the right of attack passes to the opponent. A beat on the forte (the third of the blade nearest the guard) gives the opponent the right to an immediate riposte, while a beat on the foible (the two thirds furthest from the guard) keeps priority with the attacker.

When both fencers are hit and neither has priority, the result is a [[Simultaneous Action]]. [[Épée]] is judged differently: see [[Double Touch]].

## Simultaneous Action
short: In foil and sabre, both fencers conceive and carry out an attack at the same moment. Both hits are annulled.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.89 and t.106
status: official

When both fencers are hit at once in [[Foil]] or [[Sabre]], the [[Referee]] decides whether it was a simultaneous action or a double hit. A simultaneous action comes from both fencers attacking at the same time, so no [[Touch]] is awarded to either. At foil this holds even if one of them was hit off the target. A double hit, by contrast, comes from a faulty action by one fencer and is settled by [[Priority]]. For épée, see [[Double Touch]].

## Double Touch
short: At épée, both fencers are hit and the apparatus registers both hits as valid. Each fencer has a touch scored against them.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.38, t.59, t.89, t.92, t.94 and t.106
status: official

The rule book calls this a double hit. In [[Épée]] it counts for both fencers. The exception is a [[Pool]] bout at 4 all: the fencers go on for a deciding touch and any double is not counted. If only one of the two hits is valid, only that [[Touch]] is scored.

In [[Foil]] and [[Sabre]] the same words mean something else: both fencers are hit because one of them made a faulty action, and the [[Referee]] awards one touch by [[Priority]]. If the referee cannot tell which side was at fault, the fencers are put back [[On Guard]].

> Four all in the pool and both lamps came on. Is that the bout?
> No. At four all a double is not counted. You keep fencing for the deciding touch.

## Duration of the Bout
short: The effective fencing time, counted only between "Allez!" and "Halt!". Three minutes in a pool bout, nine in direct elimination.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.37, t.38, t.39, t.40 and t.41
status: official

Only the time between each [[Allez]] and the next [[Halt]] counts. A [[Pool]] bout ends when one fencer has five [[Touch|touches]] or when three minutes have passed. A [[Direct Elimination]] bout ends at fifteen touches or after nine minutes, fenced as three periods of three minutes with a [[One-Minute Rest]] between periods. In a [[Team Event]] each bout has a limit of three minutes, and the match is won by the first team to reach the target score or by the team ahead when time expires (see [[Team Match]] and [[Relay Formula]]).

If time runs out with one fencer ahead, that fencer wins and the actual score is recorded. If the scores are level, the bout goes to a [[Deciding Touch]].

## One-Minute Rest
short: The pause of one minute between the three-minute periods of a direct elimination bout.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.39 and t.124
status: official

A [[Direct Elimination]] bout has three periods, with a rest of one minute between each. During the rest, a person named before the bout may have access to the fencer. The clock built into the scoring apparatus blocks it at the end of each period. The rest is skipped when the [[Referee]] moves to the next period because of [[Non-Combativity]] on both sides. See also [[Duration of the Bout]].

> Who goes to her at the rest?
> The person we named before the bout.

## Deciding Touch
short: The extra minute fenced for one touch when the scores are level at the end of regulation time. Lots are drawn first.
avoid: priority (for the drawing of lots; in the rules priority is about who is awarded a touch); sudden death; overtime
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.38, t.40, t.41 and t.62
status: official

When time expires with the scores level, the fencers fence for one deciding [[Touch]], with a limit of one minute. Before fencing restarts, the [[Referee]] draws lots to decide who wins if the scores are still level when the extra minute ends. This applies in [[Pool]] bouts, in [[Direct Elimination]] bouts and in the last bout of a [[Team Event]] match, where the two fencers of that last bout fence the minute. Where [[Video Refereeing]] is in use, the referee must consult the video before giving a decision on the deciding touch.

In a pool bout won on the lots, the score sheet shows the level score, for example V4 to 4.

## Non-Combativity
short: Unwillingness to fight: about one minute of fencing without a touch, or both fencers leaning back out of distance for at least 15 seconds.
avoid: passivity
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.124 and t.170
status: official

When one or both fencers make their unwillingness to fight clear, the [[Referee]] calls [[Halt]]. If it is one-sided, the inactive fencer is penalised after 15 seconds. It ends as soon as that fencer returns to the upright [[On Guard]] position. The table of offences lists it with the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]), and it applies in every kind of bout.

If it is both-sided in the first two periods of a [[Direct Elimination]] bout, the referee moves to the next period without the [[One-Minute Rest]]. In the third period the referee moves to a last minute, fenced in full, with lots drawn beforehand to decide the winner if the scores end level. In a [[Team Event]] the referee moves to the next bout, or to that same last minute if it is the last bout.

## Lifting from the Seat
short: Rising from the wheelchair seat during the bout. It is forbidden, and a touch scored while doing it is annulled.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.33, t.48 and t.170
status: official

A fencer who lifts from the seat commits an offence of the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]): a [[Yellow Card]] the first time, a [[Red Card]] after that, and any [[Touch]] scored by the fencer at fault is annulled. Raising one buttock from the seat is allowed and is not a reason to stop the bout. To enforce the rule, the [[Referee]] can call two judges to watch the fencers.

> Was that lifting? One side came off the seat.
> Raising one buttock is allowed. Lifting from the seat is not.

## Non-Sword Arm
short: The arm and hand that do not hold the weapon. They may not be used to attack or to defend.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.29, t.30, t.148 and t.170
status: official

Using the non-sword hand or arm to carry out an offensive or defensive action is an offence of the second group: the [[Touch]] scored by the fencer at fault is annulled and a [[Red Card]] is shown. Taking hold of any part of the electrical equipment with the non-sword hand during the fight is an offence of the first group, and the touch is also annulled. If the [[Referee]] notices such use, two neutral judges can be called, and the fencers can be made to change places so the fencer at fault no longer has their back to the referee. See also [[Sword Arm]] and [[Covering the Target]].

## Covering the Target
short: Protecting the valid target, or putting a non-valid part of the body in its place. An offence of the first group.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.27, t.29, t.30, t.79, t.91, t.97 and t.170
status: official

A fencer may not substitute a non-valid part of the body for the valid [[Target Area]], either by covering it or by an abnormal movement. The penalty is that of the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]), and any [[Touch]] scored by the fencer at fault is annulled. At [[Foil]], a hit that lands off the target still counts as valid when the fencer's abnormal position has put that non-valid part in place of the target. Displacing the target and ducking are allowed. Turning the back on the opponent is not.

## Losing Balance
short: When a fencer loses balance on an attack, or because the wheelchair has come unfastened, the referee stops the bout at once. No penalty.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.34, t.56 and t.170
status: official

The [[Referee]] must call [[Halt]] immediately. A [[Touch]] scored before the loss of balance is valid if the action began before the "Halt!". A touch scored after losing balance is annulled. Losing balance deliberately is an offence of the first group. Deliberately unfastening the wheelchair to force a new measurement of the [[Fencing Distance]] is an offence of the second group, punished with a [[Red Card]]. The wheelchair is held by the [[Frame]].

## Break in the Bout
short: A stop the referee may allow for wheelchair damage, for an injury or medical reason, or for a condition linked to the fencer's disability.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.35, t.36 and t.45
status: official

For a damaged or faulty wheelchair or strap, the [[Referee]] allows only the time strictly necessary to repair or exchange it, and must restart the fight after at most 10 minutes. For an injury or other medical reason attested by the World Para Fencing Medical delegate or the doctor on duty, the break lasts no longer than 10 minutes. For a condition associated with the disability, such as an involuntary spasm, the referee may allow enough time for recovery without a set limit, while making sure the rule is not used unfairly.

A fencer cannot have a second break on the same day unless it is for a different fault or a different injury. Demanding a break that the doctor judges unjustified is an offence of the second group (see [[Groups of Offences]]). Every break is noted on the score sheet of the [[Bout]].

## Yellow Card
short: A warning. The fencer now knows that any further offence in the same bout brings a penalty touch.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.132, t.162, t.165, t.168 and t.170
status: official

The [[Referee]] shows a yellow card for the first offence of the first group (see [[Groups of Offences]]). It is valid for the [[Bout]]. Any further first group offence in the same bout, the same one or a different one, is punished each time with a [[Red Card]]. In a [[Team Event]], entering the piste enclosure without the referee's permission brings a yellow card for the whole team, valid for the whole team match (see [[Team Enclosure]]). A person who is not on the [[Piste]] and disturbs good order receives a yellow card valid for the whole competition, and a [[Black Card]] the second time.

## Red Card
short: A penalty touch. One touch is added to the opponent's score.
avoid: penalty point
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.162, t.165, t.166, t.167 and t.170
status: official

A red card adds a [[Touch]] to the opponent's score, and if the last touch is at stake it can lose the [[Bout]]. It is shown for every offence of the second group, including the first one, for the first offence of the third group, and for any first group offence after a [[Yellow Card]] or after an earlier red card. A red card can only be followed by another red card or by a [[Black Card]], depending on the second offence. See [[Groups of Offences]].

> She already has a yellow. What happens if she moves before "Allez!" again?
> Red card. Her opponent gets a touch.

## Black Card
short: Exclusion from the competition and from the rest of the tournament, followed by a suspension from later World Para Fencing tournaments.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.159, t.160, t.162, t.167, t.169 and t.170
status: official

A black card is shown for the first offence of the fourth group and for a second offence of the third group in the same bout (see [[Groups of Offences]]). The suspension runs until the completion of the second World Para Fencing [[World Cup]], [[Zonal Championships|Zonal Championship]], World Championship or Paralympic Games after the tournament from which the fencer was excluded. When a team is excluded because one of its members received a black card during a team match, the team is not suspended: only that fencer is, and the fencer cannot be selected for any team during the suspension. Unlike a [[Yellow Card]] or a [[Red Card]], a black card is not limited to the bout.

## Groups of Offences
short: The four groups into which the rules sort every offence. The group decides which card is shown.
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.158 to t.170, and the World Para Fencing Offences and Penalties table, 2026
status: official

First group: a [[Yellow Card]] for the first offence, then a [[Red Card]] each time. Second group: a red card every time, including the first. Third group: a red card, then a [[Black Card]] for a second offence in the same bout. Fourth group: a black card at the first offence. A person who is not on the [[Piste]] and disturbs good order gets a yellow card first, then a black card. Penalties are cumulative and valid for the bout, except the black card. When a fencer commits several faults at once, the [[Referee]] penalises the least serious first.

Examples from the table: [[Lifting from the Seat]], [[Covering the Target]] and moving before [[Allez]] are in the first group. Use of the [[Non-Sword Arm]] and deliberately unfastening the wheelchair are in the second. Dishonest fencing is in the third. Refusing the [[Fencer's Salute]] is in the fourth. Every card is noted on the score sheet together with its group.

## Video Refereeing
short: The referee's review of an action on video, on the referee's own initiative or after an appeal by the fencer on the piste.
avoid: video analysis (for the referee's review during a bout)
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.49, t.60, t.61, t.62 and t.172
status: official

Only the fencer on the [[Piste]] may ask for a video review. In an individual event a fencer has one appeal in each [[Pool]] bout and two in each [[Direct Elimination]] bout. In a [[Team Event]] the fencers have one appeal in each bout of the match. If the [[Referee]] agrees with the fencer, the appeal is kept. The referee watches the action with the video referee consultant, with at most four repetitions, and then gives a decision that is final. The referee may also consult the monitor at any time before deciding, and must do so for a [[Deciding Touch]]. This is a refereeing procedure and is separate from the program's own [[Video Analysis]].

> Can the coach ask for the video?
> No. Only the fencer on the piste can ask.

## Team Enclosure
short: The marked area near the end of the piste reserved for a team during a team match. Often called the coaching box.
avoid: team captain (the rule book's own word for one of the two people allowed inside; program text says coaches or lead coach)
source: World Para Fencing Technical Rules, January 2026, t.131, t.132 and t.170
status: official

In a [[Team Event]] each team has an enclosure of at least 9 square metres, clearly marked on the ground between 2 and 6 metres from the end of the [[Piste]]. Besides the fencers of the [[Team Roster]], only two people have the right to be inside, one of them a coach. Team members who are not fencing must stay inside it. Nobody may enter the piste enclosure without the [[Referee]]'s permission: doing so is an offence of the first group and brings a [[Yellow Card]] for the whole team, valid for the whole match.

In individual events the rule is different. During the [[Pool]] round only fencers and referees are allowed inside the piste enclosure, and during [[Direct Elimination]] the coaches stay in a place indicated by the organisers.
