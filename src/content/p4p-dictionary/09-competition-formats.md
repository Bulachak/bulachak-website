## Directoire Technique
short: The small group of officials that runs the technical side of a competition. Usually shortened to DT.
avoid: bout committee
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.14 to o.17
status: official

The Directoire Technique checks the entries, draws up the [[Pool]] sheets and the [[Direct Elimination Table]], allocates the pistes, supervises the progress of each [[Event]] and checks the results. It has three members of different nationalities at a [[World Cup]], and six at the Paralympic Games, the Senior World Championships and Zonal Championships in Europe. It also has disciplinary responsibility, acting only as a group, and its members cannot also act as referee, competitor or delegate of their own federation at that competition.

> Who decides how many fencers go out after the pools?
> The DT. They announce it before the competition starts.

## Technical Delegate
short: The World Para Fencing representative at a competition, who checks that it meets the official criteria. Usually shortened to TD.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.13, o.16, o.22 and o.23
status: official

The Technical Delegate comes from a country other than the one hosting the [[Competition]]. At World Cups, World Championships and Zonal Championships the Technical Delegate becomes President of the [[Directoire Technique]] on arrival. Where there is no [[Refereeing Commission Delegate]] or no [[SEMI Commission Delegate]], the Technical Delegate does that job as well. For [[Zonal Championships]] the Technical Delegate is chosen from outside the zone.

## Refereeing Commission Delegate
short: The member of the World Para Fencing Refereeing Commission who organises and oversees the refereeing at a competition.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.18 and o.20
status: official

The delegates hold the referees' meeting on the day before the event, set the list of referees the day before each [[Event]], observe the referees at work and review protests. On questions about the rules, they alone may judge a referee's decision, from the beginning to the end of the [[Bout]]. A senior [[World Cup]] has one delegate. The Senior World Championships and the Paralympic Games have two, chosen from different zones. They work beside the [[Directoire Technique]] and also hold disciplinary responsibility.

## SEMI Commission Delegate
short: The member of the World Para Fencing equipment commission (SEMI) who checks the installations and oversees the control of fencers' equipment.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.18, o.19, o.28 and o.34
status: official

The SEMI Commission delegates check the technical installations and the [[Homologation]] of the equipment in use. They organise [[Weapon Control]] and the call room, and they help referees with equipment problems during matches. The organisers' timetable must leave enough time for the check of fencers' equipment: at least one day per [[Weapon]]. Where no delegate is present, the [[Technical Delegate]] takes over the role.

## Licence
short: The World Para Fencing registration a person needs for the current season in order to take part in an official competition.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.2.2, o.29.1 and o.61 to o.62; Operational Regulations, version 20251217, section 5.2
status: official

Every competitor, referee, coach and designated official at an official [[Competition]] must be licensed for the current season. The season runs from 1 January to 31 December. A fencer cannot obtain a licence before their 13th birthday. Organisers must refuse the entry of any fencer or referee without a valid licence: see [[Entry Deadline]].

## Entry Deadline
short: The last moment to enter fencers and teams by name: 42 days before the competition, through the official online entry system.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.25.1, o.56 and o.61 to o.62
status: official

Federations enter the names of their fencers, possible substitutes and teams 42 days before the first [[Event]] at the latest. After that, a fencer or team can still be added until three days before the [[Competition]] on payment of a penalty, and then until the head of delegation meeting on the day before, for a higher penalty. A withdrawal after the deadline, or a fencer who does not present to fence, brings a fine unless it is a properly proven case of force majeure. At World Cups a fencer may be replaced by another until three days before the first competition. The names of a delegation's referees are entered by the same deadline.

> Can we still add her for the World Cup?
> The 42 days have passed. Up to three days before, yes, but there is a penalty to pay.

## Entry Limit
short: The largest number of fencers and teams one federation may enter. It depends on the type of competition.
avoid: quota (an entry limit is not a quota place)
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.36.3, o.41.4, o.42.4, o.57, o.60 and o.62
status: official

At the Senior World Championships a federation may enter up to four fencers at each weapon in the individual events, and one team. At senior [[Zonal Championships]] the limit is six fencers and one team. Individual events at a [[World Cup]] have no limit, and each country may enter one team per weapon. To fence an individual discipline at the World Championships, an athlete must also have competed in at least three sanctioned competitions in that discipline in the two years before. An entry limit is separate from a [[Quota Place]].

## Minimum Participation
short: The smallest field an event needs to count as an official World Para Fencing competition.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.4.1 and o.4.3
status: official

A senior individual [[Event]] in Europe needs six fencers from at least four countries. Outside Europe it needs four fencers from at least three countries. The same four from three applies to U17, U23, Satellite and Subregional individual events, and to every [[Category C]] event. A [[Team Event]] needs three teams from three different countries. The tournament must also be attended by at least four graded referees of different nationalities.

## Fencing in a Higher Category
short: A fencer may choose to enter a category above their own. Nobody may enter a category below it.
avoid: fencing down
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.4.2 and o.108.2
status: official

A [[Category B]] fencer may enter a [[Category A]] [[Event]], and a [[Category C]] fencer may enter a Category A or Category B event. The choice must be made at the head of delegation meeting at the latest. A fencer may fence in different categories at one competition, their own or higher, but not in the same weapon. A Category C fencer who fences up, or fences in a [[Team Event]], may choose the Category C or the Category A and B [[Fencing Distance]]. Ranking points are awarded only in the category the fencer competed in.

## Pool Composition
short: How fencers are spread across the pools: by world ranking, in a back-and-forth order, keeping fencers of one country apart where possible.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.35, o.67, o.68, o.70 and o.71
status: official

With fewer than ten fencers there is a single [[Pool]]; otherwise pools have seven fencers, or seven and six, and never fewer than five. Fencers are placed from the latest official [[World Ranking]]: the top fencers go one to each pool, the next group fills the pools in the reverse order, and so on, with unranked fencers placed by drawing lots. Fencers of the same nationality go into different pools as far as possible, and when they share a pool they fence each other first. The pools are displayed by 6 p.m. local time on the day before.

## Pool Indicators
short: The three figures that rank every fencer after the pools: victories divided by bouts, touches scored minus touches received, then touches scored.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.73
status: official

After the [[Pool|Pools]] one single ranking is made of all the fencers who took part. The first figure is victories divided by bouts fenced, with 1 as the highest value. Ties are separated by the difference between [[Touch|Touches]] scored and touches received, then by touches scored. Fencers who are still exactly level are separated by drawing lots. The rules call these figures indices, and the ranking they give seeds the [[Direct Elimination Table]].

> We both won four. Why is she seeded above me?
> Her touches scored minus touches received is better. That is the second indicator.

## Pool Elimination Rate
short: The share of fencers who go out after the pools. At senior World Cups and Zonal Championships it may not be more than 20 percent.
avoid: the cut
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.72 and o.73.3
status: official

The [[Directoire Technique]] decides and announces the number before the competition starts, from the ranking made by the [[Pool Indicators]]. At senior [[World Cup|World Cups]] and [[Zonal Championships]] the rate is chosen so that at least a complete table of 16 remains. When an event has 16 fencers or fewer, nobody is eliminated. Nobody is eliminated after the pools at Satellite Tournaments, Subregional Championships or Games, U17 and U23 events, or in [[Category C]] events. If fencers are exactly level for the last qualifying place, all of them qualify.

## Exemption from Pools
short: At senior World Cups and Zonal Championships, the highest ranked fencers entered skip the pools and go straight into the direct elimination table.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.86 to o.89
status: official

The number of exemptions depends on the entries: none with fewer than 16 fencers, 4 with 16 to 21, 6 with 22 to 27, 8 with 28 to 33, and so on up to 16 with 52 or more. The exemptions go by the most recent official [[World Ranking]]. Exempt fencers take the first places in the [[Direct Elimination Table]] in ranking order, and the fencers who qualify from the [[Pool|Pools]] follow them. If an exempt fencer does not present to fence, the place in the table stays empty and the fencer receives no ranking points. The list of exempt fencers is published with the pools on the day before.

## Direct Elimination Table
short: The bout plan for the knockout rounds. It sets who meets whom from the first round to the final.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.76, o.77, o.80, o.82, o.85, o.90, o.93 and o.98
status: official

The table is built from the ranking after the [[Pool|Pools]], and the rule that keeps fencers of one country apart is not applied. In each round (128, 64, 32, 16, 8 or 4) the [[Bout|Bouts]] are called in the order of the plan, from the top to the bottom. In the formulas with pools there is no bout for third place: the two fencers who lose the semi-finals are placed equal third. Fencers who lose in the same round are ranked by their seeding in the table. In a [[Team Event]] the first four teams are placed by the world team ranking, the other ranked teams are drawn in pairs, and unranked teams take the last places by lot.

## Repechage
short: A second table for fencers who have lost once. It is used in individual events at the World Championships and the Paralympic Games.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.66, o.95 and o.108.2
status: official

At the World Championships repechage starts from the table of 16, and at the Paralympic Games from the table of 8. The [[Direct Elimination Table]] is built from the [[World Ranking]], with positions drawn in pairs (1 and 2, 3 and 4, and so on). Fencers who lose in the main table drop into the repechage table, and a second loss eliminates them. At the Paralympic Games the two winners of the second repechage round fence for third place. At the World Championships the two winners of the fourth repechage round are placed equal third.

## Individual Event Formula
short: The structure of an individual event. The rules set four, named A to D, by type of competition.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.66, o.83, o.84, o.86, o.88, o.91 and o.95
status: official

Formula A, for U17 and U23 events, is one round of [[Pool|Pools]] with nobody eliminated, then a [[Direct Elimination Table]]. Formula B, for senior [[World Cup|World Cups]] and senior [[Zonal Championships]], is one qualifying round of pools with [[Exemption from Pools]], then direct elimination. Formula C, for Satellite Tournaments and Subregional Championships or Games, is one round of pools for every fencer present, then direct elimination. Formula D, for the Paralympic Games and the Senior World Championships, is direct elimination with [[Repechage]]. In formulas A, B and C the pools and the direct elimination are fenced on the same day.

## Team Match
short: The whole series of bouts between two teams. In the men's and women's formula it is six bouts, up to 30 touches.
avoid: relay (for the match); team bout
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.5, o.6 and o.99
status: official

Each team fences with three fencers: two in the [[Category A]] positions and one in the [[Category B]] position. A fencer meets only the opponents in the same category position: the Category A fencers of one team meet each Category A fencer of the other, and the two Category B fencers meet twice, in a fixed order of six bouts. Each bout adds five [[Touch|Touches]] to the target and lasts three minutes at most, under the [[Relay Formula]]. The team that first reaches 30, or that leads when the time is up, wins. Each team decides which of its fencers takes which position.

## Team Category Composition
short: The mix of categories a team must put on the piste. A men's or women's team must fence at least one Category B athlete.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.43, o.97, o.99.10 and o.104.2
status: official

A men's or women's team has three fencers, with or without a reserve, all of the same nationality, and at least one [[Category B]] athlete must fence. A Category B athlete may be chosen for a [[Category A]] position, and a [[Category C]] athlete for the Category B position. A mixed team has four fencers and no reserve: one Category A man, one Category B man, one Category A woman and one Category B woman. A team may begin a [[Team Match]] only if it is complete. The named athletes are the [[Team Roster]].

> Can we fence three Category A men in the sabre team?
> No. At least one Category B athlete has to fence.

## Mixed Team Formula
short: The team format with two men and two women. Four bouts, each against the opponent of the same gender and category, up to 20 touches.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.43 and o.104
status: official

Each team has one [[Category A]] man, one [[Category B]] man, one Category A woman and one Category B woman, with no reserve. The four bouts are scored with the [[Relay Formula]], up to a maximum of 20 [[Touch|Touches]]. The order is settled by coin tosses before each match: the winner of the first toss chooses the gender and category of the first bout, the other team chooses the second, and a toss by the [[Directoire Technique]] settles the third and fourth. If a fencer cannot continue, the team loses the match. At the Paralympic Games the foil and épée [[Team Event|Team Events]] are mixed.

## Team Substitution
short: Bringing the reserve into a men's or women's team match. Only one substitution is allowed per team per match.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.61.1, o.99.10 and o.104.7
status: official

A team may ask for a substitution before a given bout of the [[Team Match]]. For an injury or other medical reason recognised by the medical delegate or the doctor on duty, it can be immediate, even during a bout. A fencer replaced because of an injury may not fence again in that match. After a substitution there must still be at least one [[Category B]] or [[Category C]] fencer among the three on the [[Piste]]. If both a fencer and the reserve have to retire, the team loses the match, and the [[Mixed Team Formula]] has no substitute at all.

## Ranking Points Scale
short: The points each final placing earns for the world ranking: 32 for first in an individual event, 64 for a winning team, before the coefficient.
avoid: multiplier (the rules say factor, this dictionary says coefficient)
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.108.2 and o.109.2
status: official

In an individual [[Event]] first place earns 32 points, second 26, equal third 20, places 5 to 8 earn 14, places 9 to 16 earn 8, places 17 to 32 earn 4, places 33 to 64 earn 2 and places 65 to 96 earn 1. In a [[Team Event]] first place earns 64, second 52, third 40 and fourth 36. The points are then weighted by the [[Coefficient]] of the competition: 0.1 for Subregional Championships or Games, 0.3 for a Satellite Tournament, 1.0 for a [[World Cup]], 1.2 for [[Zonal Championships]], 2.0 for World Championships and 3.0 for the Paralympic Games. Events with [[Repechage]] have their own lines for places 5 to 16. The weighted points feed the [[World Ranking]].

## Counted Results
short: The results that make up a world ranking total: the best six senior World Cups, plus the latest championships. A result drops out after two years at most.
source: World Para Fencing Organisation Rules, January 2026, o.108.1 and o.109.1
status: official

The senior individual [[World Ranking]] counts a fencer's best six [[World Cup]] results, best three Satellite Tournament results, the latest World Championships or Paralympic Games, the latest [[Zonal Championships]] and the best Subregional result. The team ranking counts the same, without Satellite Tournaments. A competition in the current season replaces the same competition from two seasons before, and any result is cancelled on its second anniversary at the latest. Fencers level on points are separated by the number of first places, then second places, and so on. The points themselves come from the [[Ranking Points Scale]].
